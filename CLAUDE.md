# CLAUDE.md

Godot 4.7 (GDScript, Forward+, Jolt physics) third-person exploration prototype on a procedurally
generated tropical island. See README.md for features and controls.

## Running and verifying

Godot binary: `/Applications/Godot.app/Contents/MacOS/Godot`. There is no test suite, so verify
changes by running the game. The `godot-verify` skill (`.claude/skills/godot-verify/`) has the
full workflow. In short:

```sh
# Re-import after adding or moving assets, or after editing *.import files or world/*.exr
Godot --headless --path . --import

# Re-bake the island from code (overwrites hand edits to world/*.exr)
Godot --headless --path . --script res://tools/bake_island.gd -- --seed=71195 --force

# Screenshots of standard views + fps/draw-call stats (windowed: headless can't render)
Godot --path . --audio-driver Dummy --resolution 1280x720 \
  --script res://tools/screenshot.gd -- --out=<dir> --seed=12345
```

- Always pass `--audio-driver Dummy` so test runs don't play sound on the user's speakers.
- `--headless` uses a dummy renderer: shaders aren't compiled and screenshots are blank. Use
  it for logic checks, and a windowed run for anything visual.
- Use a fixed `--seed` when comparing before/after.
- Close the Godot editor before moving or renaming files outside it.
- Temporary test scripts must live inside the project (`res://`). Delete them and their `.uid`
  afterwards.
- "N resources still in use at exit" and "ObjectDB instances leaked" at the end of
  `--script` runs are harness noise, not game bugs.

## Architecture

Everything is built at runtime from `scripts/world/world_generator.gd` (`_ready`). The terrain
data either comes from the **baked island** (`baked_island` → `world/island.tres`, an
`island_data.gd` resource with float EXR heightmap and masks, loaded by `_load_baked`), or is
generated by `generate()`, which runs steps 1–4 on the main island's grid (`resolution`),
then grows the grid to `world_resolution` (`_expand_world`, main island kept unchanged in
the middle) and adds the islet (`_add_islet`):

1. `_generate_heightmap`: warped fBm + ridged noise + island falloff → `heights` (0..1, row-major
   `z * size + x`). It also records `island_edge`.
2. `_erode`: droplet hydraulic erosion, which also fills `flow_map` and `deposit_map`
   (passed to the terrain shader as vertex colours).
3. `_shape_beaches`: a monotonic height remap that flattens the band around sea level.
   `_sink_seabed` then drops the seabed away from the coast.
4. `water_carver.gd` (`carve(gen)`): picks a lake site, routes a river to the sea (Dijkstra with
   an uphill penalty, then smoothing and meanders), and works out a never-rising, slope-limited
   surface profile. It carves the bed and shapes the banks (rims and levees stay above the
   water past the water mesh's edge), and fills `water_height` (water surface per vertex: sea
   level outside the lake and river) and `inland_mask`. With `waterfall` on, a second outlet
   heads along `waterfall_bearing` (±30°, scored by drop and steepness): a gorge to a sheer
   lip, a plunge pool (or the sea, if the cliff foot is at sea level) and on to the sea. The
   falls go in `gen.waterfalls` ({top, dir, drop, width, cave, ledge}). With `waterfall_cave`,
   it also raises a ledge in the heightmap behind the curtain.
   Channels share `_profile` and `_carve_channel` (with a `keep` filter, used to leave a
   vertical face at the lip).
4. `_build_terrain`: mesh with analytic normals (`normal_map`) + `HeightMapShape3D`
   collision (uniformly scaled by `cell_size`). Cells deeper than `HIDDEN_SEABED_DEPTH`
   get no triangles (the sea is opaque there), which keeps the big open-sea grid cheap.
5. (`day_night.gd` is set up first, at the start of `_ready`.) `_build_water`, `_place_player`, then `vegetation.gd` (`build(gen)`),
   `ambient_audio.gd` (`setup(gen, vegetation)`) and `build/builder.gd` (`setup(gen, player)`).

`tools/bake_island.gd` runs `generate()` and writes the EXRs and the `.tres`, including the RNG
state, so vegetation matches exactly. Without `--force` it refuses to overwrite, to protect
hand edits. The baked files are the source of truth for the shipped island: generator changes
only take effect after a re-bake. Masks channel B holds `inland_mask`; `island_water.exr` holds
`water_height`; the `.tres` holds `waterfalls` and `islets`. Bakes without the water file load as sea-only.

The generator is the source of truth for terrain queries. Use these rather than re-deriving:
`get_height(x, z)` (same triangle split as the mesh), `grid_index`, `surface_at`,
`water_surface_at` (lake, river or sea level at a point; sea level wherever `inland_mask` shows no water, because the
carver writes lake and river levels a little past their banks), `water_depth_at`, `water_current_at`
`inland_water_at` and `islet_weight` (vegetation thins out on islets). `water_surface()` is the sea level only. Use it just for sea-specific
things (beaches, surf).

`_build_inland_water` builds one mesh over every cell that touches inland water, using the
per-vertex water heights. Vertex colour carries the downstream direction and speed for
`shaders/inland_water.gdshader`, which does flow-map ripples and white water. Cells where the
water surface drops more than `WATERFALL_STEP` are left out: `waterfall.gd` draws each fall
there (a ballistic curtain mesh with `waterfall.gdshader`, foam on the pool, mist
particles, and the river-rush loop pitched down as the roar).

- `cave.gd`: the grotto behind a waterfall. The heightmap can't hold a cave, so `layout()`
  picks a box of terrain cells that `_build_terrain` leaves out (mesh quads skipped,
  collision vertices set to NaN, which Jolt treats as holes). `build()` fills the box from a
  signed distance field (under the heightmap, minus tunnel and chamber) meshed with
  surface nets. Outside the cave the vertices sit exactly on the heightmap, using its normals
  and snapped to the box sides, so there are no seams. It reuses the terrain material:
  COLOR.a < 0.99 marks cave rock (no grass) and fades ambient light (AO) with depth.
  `gen.is_underground(pos)` (below the heightmap) makes `water_depth_at` return 0 and stops
  swimming in caves under rivers. Footsteps on `CaveBody` are stone, and you can't build
  underground. Drips (`audio/cave/`) play at random spots, mostly at the pool. An `Echo` Area3D
  sends sounds made in the cave to a reverb bus (`Cave`, created in code). Deeper in, the
  `Ambience` bus (all outdoor sound, including the waterfall roar) is turned down and low-passed,
  following the camera. Keep new outdoor sounds on `Ambience` and close-up effects on `SFX`.
- `birds.gd`: seabird flocks, one MultiMesh moved on the CPU each frame. Each flock circles
  a drifting point over the shore, picked by farthest-point sampling of the coast, with the
  first one at the waterfall. Birds bank toward the centre and stay `TERRAIN_CLEARANCE` above
  the ground. `shaders/bird.gdshader` flaps the wings from INSTANCE_CUSTOM (phase, flap
  amount, rate); UV2.x is the distance out along the wing. Each flock has one voice
  (AudioStreamPlayer3D on `Ambience`). A random member calls every few seconds and the voice
  follows it. A flock's `members` is a plain Array: a packed array inside a Dictionary is
  copied on access, so appending to it changes nothing.
- `beach_birds.gd`: gulls in small groups on low, flat sand (farthest-point sampling of
  beach vertices). A state machine runs stand / walk / peck, then on spooking (player within
  `SPOOK_DISTANCE`, doubled when running) take-off, fly and land. Neighbours panic after a
  short delay, and after fleeing a bird lands on a beach 50–200 m away, out of the player's
  reach. Only birds within `SIMULATE_RANGE` of the player, or in the air, are updated. It shares
  the gull mesh (`birds.gd` `_bird_mesh`, which has legs) and `bird.gdshader`, where
  INSTANCE_CUSTOM.a folds the wings and drops the legs, and .b is the walking speed on the
  ground. Only wing triangles carry UV2.x: anything with UV2.x > 0 flaps and folds.
- Berries (`vegetation.gd`, `proc_mesh.gd`): `bush()` stores its leaf balls as mesh meta
  `blobs`, and `berry_cluster` hangs berry bunches on their outside, with the leaf cards' sway
  weights (`BUSH_SWAY`), drawn with the bush's own transform (`shaders/fruit.gdshader`:
  albedo is the vertex COLOR, written linear by `_fruit_ball`). `BERRY_CHANCE` of bushes get one, from their own RNG (`berry_rng`).
  pickups.gd splits them into MultiMeshes per bush shape and `CHUNK_SIZE` chunk, so they can
  have a visibility range (`BERRY_RANGE`); picking hides the cluster and the player holds
  `berry_handful_mesh`.
- Coconuts (`vegetation.gd`, `proc_mesh.gd`): `palm()` stores its crown position as mesh meta
  (`crown`). Each palm shape gets a matching `coconut_cluster` mesh, drawn with the palm's own
  transform and `CROWN_SWAY` as its sway weight, so it moves exactly with the top of the trunk
  in the bark shader's wind. Coconut placement uses its own RNG (`coco_rng`), so it never shifts
  the tree layout. Keep it that way for future additions to the scatter passes.
- Rocks stay off tree trunks: the tree pass records each trunk (`_add_trunk`, in a
  `TRUNK_CELL` grid; palms with their lean) and the rock pass drops any rock that
  `_hits_trunk`. The check comes after the rock's random rolls, so dropping one doesn't shift
  the rest.
- Mangoes and oranges (`vegetation.gd`, `proc_mesh.gd`): `broadleaf()` stores its canopy
  blobs (`blobs`, Vector4) and sway curve (`sway_k`: weight = y² × sway_k) as mesh meta.
  `tree_fruit_cluster` builds one cluster per tree shape × canopy blob × fruit, hung at the
  blob's outer rim (other blobs' leaves would hide it), with meta `nuts` and `fruit`, drawn
  with the tree's own transform. `FRUIT_TREE_CHANCE` of rainforest trees bear one fruit, with
  a cluster under each blob `FRUIT_BLOB_CHANCE` of the time, from `fruit_rng`.
- `pickups.gd`: things the player can pick up or knock down. Vegetation hands over the fallen
  coconuts, loose rocks (the "odd lone rock" scatter branch, with their collider owner ids),
  the fruit bunches (coconuts, mangoes, oranges) and the berry bushes' berries, so they're out
  of its chunked batches.
  An item's `reach` is how far its edge is from its origin (rocks, bushes): the prompt and the
  pick spot measure from there. There's one MultiMesh per kind
  (mesh). Rocks give `STONES_PER_METRE` × size stones, and the player lifts a hand-sized stone off
  the rock. `knock(point)` drops a bunch's fruit (meta `nuts` on the cluster mesh), which falls
  and then becomes pickable; each fruit's fallen MultiMesh (`fruit_kinds`) pools its slots. `bunch_on_line` is the aim assist
  for throws, since bunches have no collision. For each item,
  E calls `player.pick_up`, which walks to a spot set by `PICK_REACH`, plays the pick clip and
  calls back: on the grab the instance is hidden (zero scale) and the player draws the item on
  a `RightHand` BoneAttachment3D; when it's put away it goes into `player.inventory`. It grows back after
  `REGROW_TIME` when the player is out of sight range. The prompt and toast use the builder's
  HUD. The builder's menu has an Inventory page.
- `player/hotbar.gd`: six slots filled automatically from the inventory with EQUIPPABLE ids.
  Keys 1–6 select, and the same key again holsters. The held item is drawn at `player.hand`
  as a top-level node, because the hand bone carries the model's 0.338 scale. Clicking throws a
  stone: a RigidBody aimed at the camera ray hit (or a bunch on the line), with an arc
  compensating for gravity. In flight it calls `pickups.knock`, and on its first contact it
  calls `beach_birds.spook`. The bar fades after `SHOW_TIME`; a corner reminder stays while
  something is held. An aiming dot sits at the view centre (where throws go) while a THROWABLE
  item is held, ringed when `bunch_on_line` finds a bunch.
- `vegetation.gd`: jittered-grid scattering into MultiMeshes chunked every `CHUNK_SIZE` m (a
  fixed size, so culling doesn't degrade on a bigger world), plus one shared
  `StaticBody3D` with shape owners for collision. Grass is a camera-following GPU field:
  `grass.gdshader` reads the heightmap texture and a density mask.
- `proc_mesh.gd`: procedural palms, broadleaf trees, bushes, rocks, grass clumps and leaf
  textures. Vertex conventions: `UV2.x` = wind sway weight, `UV2.y` = leaf flutter,
  `COLOR.r` = per-leaf variation.
- `shaders/wind.gdshaderinc`: the shared wind model, used by grass, foliage and bark so they
  move together.
- `scripts/build/`: crafting and placement. `recipes.gd` lists recipes: scene, cost, post
  footprint, clearance box. `inventory.gd` is the player's resources (`player.inventory`);
  `free_build` is on until gathering exists. `builder.gd` has the M interaction menu (pages
  of rows built in `_open_menu`; context rows carry a `target` that's highlighted while
  selected), the preview and its
  checks (dry, unevenness, a physics box query for clearance), and placement. It stretches
  meshes named `Post*` down to the ground and generates convex collision from the other
  meshes (skipping `Lash*`). Placed builds go under `Structures` (with a `recipe` meta) and
  aren't saved yet. The menu's "Take down" row removes the build the camera faces and
  refunds it. Optional recipe keys: `reach` (preview distance), `max_slope` (no posts: tilt to the
  ground instead of the unevenness check), `limit` (how many can be placed), `about` (menu
  text), `grass_clearing` (radius of trimmed grass, via `vegetation.set_grass_clearings`,
  up to 16 spots in `grass.gdshader`). A placed scene's root can have `built()` (called after
  placing; the preview never gets it) and `menu_actions()` (extra rows on the root page
  while it's faced; the menu refreshes after each).
- `build/radio.gd` (`models/radio.tscn`, a free recipe with `limit` 1): a playlist from
  `audio/radio/` (track1, track2, each with a `volume` trim to even out loudness) played in
  order and repeated, so the tracks must not loop on import (`edit/loop_mode=1`). Switching on
  lands where the clock says the playlist is ("live"); skipping starts the next song. Both play
  tuning static first. 3D on a `Radio` bus made in code (band-pass, EQ, compressor,
  `BUS_BOOST` gain and a limiter), which sends to `Ambience`. It needs a big `unit_size` and the
  boost to carry over the surf.
- `shaders/sky.gdshader`: the sky (the scene's Sky material). It reproduces
  ProceduralSkyMaterial's gradient and sun, plus 2D fBm clouds projected onto a flat layer
  (no ray marching), stars and the moon. Things that move every frame come from global
  shader parameters (`project.godot` `[shader_globals]`): `cloud_time` (set by
  `world_generator.gd` `_process`), `sun_direction` and `moon_direction`. Don't use TIME or
  the LIGHT0_* built-ins in the sky shader: either makes Godot re-render the radiance
  cubemap every frame, about three times the cost (≈0.6 ms vs 0.2 ms on an M1 Pro at
  720p). Global parameters don't trigger that; material uniforms do.
- `day_night.gd` (`gen.day_night`): the clock (`hour`, `clock()`), sun and moon. A day
  takes `day_length_minutes` (generator export), and night hours run `night_speed` times
  faster. The scene's one DirectionalLight3D is the sun by day and the moon by night,
  swapping while both are low at dusk and dawn (one shadow-casting light). Moonlight is
  faked to come from at least `MOON_LIGHT_MIN_HEIGHT`, and a flat `NIGHT_AMBIENT` mixes in,
  so nights stay playable. Colours come from palettes keyed by the sun's height; the sky's
  material uniforms are set only every `SKY_UPDATE` s (each set re-renders the cubemap).
  `night` (0..1) is for other systems (ambient_audio.gd fades the birdsong). Unshaded
  materials don't darken at night by themselves: call `DAY_NIGHT.dim_at_night(material)`
  for a StandardMaterial3D, or read `global uniform vec3 daylight` in a shader (fruit glow,
  splash rings). `tools/screenshot.gd --hour=21` captures a fixed time of day.
- `scripts/ui/compass.gd`: the compass bar at the top centre (a Control on the builder's HUD,
  drawn in `_draw` every frame). Bearings match the map (0° = north = -Z). Placed structures
  show as markers with their distance; the clock is on the right.
- `scripts/ui/map_view.gd`: the map (opened from the interaction menu). Packs heights, the
  inland mask and water heights into a float texture for `shaders/map.gdshader`, which does
  the colouring, hill shading and contours; markers are drawn on a Control on top. It pauses
  the tree while open, so it runs with `PROCESS_MODE_ALWAYS`.
- `scripts/player/player.gd`: CharacterBody3D controller, an AnimationTree built in code
  (ground/air/swim transition), footsteps and splashes, swimming. `footprints.gd` holds a pooled,
  fading Decal trail that sand steps leave behind. `water_splash.gd` is the same idea for steps in
  shallow water: pooled one-shot droplet bursts (GPUParticles3D) plus foam rings
  (`shaders/splash_ring.gdshader`, aged through an instance uniform), scaled by speed and depth. Swim strokes
  splash at the entering hand (LeftHand/RightHand bone, alternating with `stroke_phase`). Swimming plays three hand-keyed clips
  (`characters/human/swim_animations.res`): tread when still, and when moving breast
  (breaststroke) or crawl while sprinting. A `stroke` Blend2 sits inside the tread/moving one, and
  the body pitch goes from `BREAST_PITCH` to `CRAWL_PITCH`. Each clip is sought to a phase the
  controller advances, so splashes land on hand entry (breaststroke: both hands at `BREAST_SPLASH`). Sitting (C) plays
  `sit_animations.res`: `down` (sought forwards, then backwards to stand up) and a seated
  `idle` loop. Picking up plays `pick_animations.res` (`up`; the right hand reaches `GRAB` in
  `pick_clips.py`, which must match `PICK_REACH`). Throwing (`hotbar.gd` calls `player.throw`)
  overlays `throw_animations.res` on the upper body only (a filtered Blend2 after the state
  machine), turning to the aim; the stone launches from the hand at `THROW_RELEASE`. All are authored in `player-swim.blend`
  (outside the repo) by the text blocks versioned in `tools/blender/`, and converted with
  `tools/convert_anims.gd`, which maps the root bone between the glb and FBX skeletons and
  checks the result against Idle. The `player-animation` skill has the full
  workflow for adding or fixing clips.

## Conventions and gotchas

- **No `class_name`**: cross-script references use `const X = preload("res://…")`, so scripts
  work in `--script` runs without the editor's class cache. Accessing members through an
  untyped reference (e.g. `gen` in vegetation.gd) gives Variants, so declare those locals with
  explicit types (`var h: float = gen.get_height(...)`). `:=` fails to infer.
- **Name collisions with built-ins**: don't name methods after Node methods (`get_index` broke
  the build) or locals after Node3D properties (`basis`, `position`) or global functions
  (`seed`, `len`). When renaming a shadowing local, update every use; a leftover silently
  reads the node's property instead.
- **Visibility range on a MultiMesh** is measured to the middle of the whole MultiMesh, not to each
  instance. Only use it on spatially chunked batches (vegetation's `CHUNK_SIZE` chunks). On one
  MultiMesh spread over the world, such as the birds or pickups, it hides everything.
- **Keep terrain sampling consistent**: CPU `_sample`, the grass shader's `terrain_height` and
  the mesh all split each cell along the same diagonal. Change all three together.
- **Import settings are part of the source**. Edit the `.import` file, then re-import:
  - Ambience WAVs need `edit/loop_mode=2`.
  - Palette PNGs need `detect_3d/compress_to=0`, or VRAM compression smears the colours.
- **Commit the `.import` and `.uid` files**; never commit `.godot/`.
- Style: tabs, typed GDScript, `##` doc comments on scripts and non-obvious functions. Tunables
  go in `@export` groups on the generator or in named constants.
- Third-party assets must be CC0 and recorded in `audio/CREDITS.txt` or next to the asset. The
  `cc0-assets` skill has the sourcing and processing pipeline.
- The user's shell is zsh: unquoted `$var` does not word-split, and `$1:c` is a history
  modifier (write `${1}`).
